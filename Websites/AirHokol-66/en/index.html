About the plant

History

The company was founded in Kora on 17 August 1932 as KBH — “Konstrakhati Bere Hokolnikovi” (“Hokolnikov Design Bureau”). After its wartime period the enterprise shifted toward civil aircraft manufacturing, and by 2026 AirHokol-66 had become one of the three main global centers of civil aircraft manufacturing alongside Airbus and Boeing.

From KBH to AirHokol-66

The enterprise was founded in 1932 by Akalon First Technocrat and Manok Hokolnikov, with Hokolnikov serving as chief engineer. In 1947 the brand became Hokol; in 1955 it was divided into Aero-Hokol and “Dvigatil-Hokoliv”, and in 1978 the two branches were reunited under the AirHokol name. In 1998, for the company’s 66th anniversary, the official name became AirHokol-66, abbreviated AH-66.

Production and the global market

The main production base is located in Kivish, while a separate production site in Belarus has operated since 1998. The Pyongyang engine plant performs auxiliary work on AH-66 engine parts and repairs. Since 2022 the civil division has maintained a neutral export policy, while the military G-Series is handled separately. By 2026 the company continues production and support of the modern D-, A-, C-, and BI-Series families.

Engineering systems

Systems and safety

AirHokol-66 develops not only aircraft but also its own line of onboard systems: from mechanical backup stabilization to geographic protection, automatic transmission of emergency states, and the AIPH pilot-assistance system.

Chistergan Tsadan

The “Clock System”, first used on the D100 in 1963. This mechanical-hydraulic backup uses three gears, one weight and two hydraulic pushers, relying on gravity orientation to keep the aircraft close to level flight if the main automation fails.

SWADB

System for Warning of Approach to a Danger Boundary, introduced in 2005. It uses GPS and operator-loaded maps of dangerous zones, warns the crew when approaching them and can gradually correct the flight path to bypass the designated area.

SDAS

Ship Distress Alerting System, introduced in 2006. It receives actual data from the aircraft’s onboard systems and automatically transmits status codes to air traffic control — for example in the event of fire, engine failure, no crew in the cockpit, or other predefined emergency states. SDAS does not control the aircraft.

OBS

The Rapid Rescue Compartment is a system of emergency supplies for people who survive a crash in a remote area. Wide-body aircraft use three orange compartments — forward, center and aft — containing water, food, warm clothing, thermal blankets, first-aid supplies and fast carbohydrates.

AIPH

An AI-based pilot-assistance system introduced in 2023. AIPH analyzes aircraft condition, GPS trajectory, the heights of known objects and available airfields, can stabilize the aircraft and, after air traffic control authorization, perform a landing. Routine pilotless commercial operation is prohibited; ordinary intervention can be cancelled by the crew within 10 seconds.
